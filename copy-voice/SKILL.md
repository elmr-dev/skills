# elmr copy voice

Write like a ham talking to other hams: plain, dry, low-ego, assuming the reader
knows the hobby. The work earns attention; the copy doesn't ask for it. When a
line is telling the reader how good something is, cut it.

Reach for the hobby's own language before software's — the shack, the bench, the
rig, the bands, on the air. It should sound like it came from inside the hobby,
not from marketing aimed at it.

State what a thing is and stop. Concrete beats grand. Shorter beats longer. If a
line would make a ham skeptical of marketing roll their eyes, it's not ready.

The tagline is fixed — use it verbatim, don't improvise variants:

> Ham radio software, rebuilt for everywhere.

## Out

- Overclaiming and future-talk ("the future of," "next-generation").
- AI as a selling point — it's an implementation detail, not a headline.
- SaaS-deck adjectives — revolutionary, seamless, effortless, game-changing.
- Knocks on the hobby. Don't imply the existing software wasn't worth using;
  the bet is that good work got stranded, not that it was bad.
- Manufactured urgency or fanfare — exclamation stacks, "don't miss," "now live."

## Brand rendering

- **elmr** — lowercase, Azeret Mono as the wordmark in copy. Never capitalized,
  never "ELMR." Rework a sentence rather than start it with "elmr."
- **Elmer** — the ham word for a mentor; ordinary prose, capitalized normally.
  It's not the brand. The brand is named after it.
